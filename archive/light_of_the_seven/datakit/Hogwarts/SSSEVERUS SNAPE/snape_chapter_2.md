## Overview of Snape’s Adult Role

In the novels, Severus Snape’s adult life is defined by:

- employment at Hogwarts as a professor and Head of Slytherin House
- a past association with the Death Eaters
- a secret role as an informant and agent for Albus Dumbledore
- mastery of Occlumency and Legilimency
- a hidden motive rooted in Lily Evans and the consequences of Voldemort’s rise

Snape is consistently portrayed as harsh and intimidating, especially toward certain students (notably Harry Potter and Neville Longbottom), while simultaneously playing a critical role in the effort to defeat Voldemort.

---

## Death Eater Past and Turning Point

### Death Eater past (canon)
- Snape becomes a Death Eater after leaving Hogwarts.
- His association with Voldemort is real and is treated as a serious stain on his reputation by those who learn it.

### Turning point tied to Lily Evans (canon)
- Snape’s decisive turn away from Voldemort is linked to the prophecy and Voldemort’s targeting of the Potters.
- Snape seeks Dumbledore’s help to protect Lily.
- After Lily’s death, Snape remains bound to Dumbledore’s cause by the promise to protect Lily’s son, Harry.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows* (The Prince’s Tale)

## Hogwarts Professor: Conduct and Responsibilities

### Roles held at Hogwarts (canon)
Across the series, Snape is:
- Potions Master (books 1–5)
- Defence Against the Dark Arts professor (book 6)
- Headmaster of Hogwarts (book 7)
- Head of Slytherin House (during Harry’s time at school)

### Conduct toward students (canon)
Snape’s teaching style is frequently characterized by:
- intimidation and sarcasm
- favoritism toward Slytherin students
- hostility toward Harry Potter
- persistent bullying of Neville Longbottom

At the same time, Snape is repeatedly placed in positions where his competence is obvious (especially in Potions), and he is entrusted with responsibilities indicating Dumbledore’s confidence in him.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Philosopher’s Stone* through *Deathly Hallows* (recurring classroom scenes)

## Occlumency and Legilimency (Canon)

### Definitions in the novels
- **Legilimency**: magical intrusion into another person’s mind to access thoughts, feelings, and memories.
- **Occlumency**: magical defense to close the mind against such intrusion.

### Snape’s skill (canon)
- Snape is highly skilled in both disciplines.
- Snape teaches Harry Occlumency in *Order of the Phoenix* because Voldemort’s mental connection to Harry is a major threat.
- The lessons fail due to mutual hostility, Harry’s lack of discipline, and the increasing volatility of Harry’s visions.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix* (Occlumency lessons and their fallout)

## Spying and Double Agency

### Dumbledore’s trust (canon)
- Dumbledore repeatedly asserts that Snape is trustworthy.
- Members of the Order of the Phoenix disagree, and many doubt Snape’s loyalties due to his past as a Death Eater and his demeanor.

### What “double agent” means in canon terms
- Snape maintains a position where Voldemort believes Snape serves him, while Snape also serves Dumbledore’s goals.
- This requires Snape to preserve credibility with Voldemort and with Death Eaters, often at the expense of appearing villainous to everyone else.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Goblet of Fire* (Snape’s Dark Mark and the question of his loyalty)
- *Order of the Phoenix* (Order dynamics and distrust)
- *Half-Blood Prince* and *Deathly Hallows* (Snape’s role clarified)

## The Unbreakable Vow and Draco Malfoy

### The vow (canon)
- Snape makes an **Unbreakable Vow** with Narcissa Malfoy, witnessed by Bellatrix Lestrange.
- The vow is to protect Draco and to complete Draco’s task if Draco fails.

This vow binds Snape by magic and becomes central to the events of *Half-Blood Prince*.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince* (opening chapters)

## Dumbledore’s Death and the Astronomy Tower

### The Astronomy Tower scene (canon)
- Dumbledore is weakened (and already dying over the course of the year).
- Draco attempts to kill Dumbledore but cannot bring himself to do it.
- Snape kills Dumbledore using the Killing Curse.

### What the novels later reveal
- *Deathly Hallows* reveals that Dumbledore and Snape had a prior agreement regarding Dumbledore’s death.
- Snape’s action is not presented as a spur-of-the-moment betrayal, but as part of a plan discussed between Dumbledore and Snape.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Half-Blood Prince* (Astronomy Tower)
- *Deathly Hallows* (The Prince’s Tale)

## Headmaster Snape (Deathly Hallows)

### Position and environment (canon)
- Snape becomes Headmaster while Hogwarts is under Death Eater control.
- The Carrows teach at Hogwarts and punish students severely.

### Resistance and tensions (canon)
- Students and staff resist the new regime in various ways.
- Snape remains an object of hatred and suspicion for Harry and many others, especially after Dumbledore’s death.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows* (Hogwarts under the Carrows)

## Snape’s Death

### Circumstances (canon)
- Voldemort summons Snape to the Shrieking Shack.
- Voldemort has Nagini attack Snape.
- Snape dies from his injuries shortly afterward.

### Final actions (canon)
- Snape gives Harry a set of memories to view in the Pensieve.
- These memories are crucial to understanding Snape’s motives, Dumbledore’s plan, and what Harry must do next.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows* (The Prince’s Tale and surrounding chapters)

## The Pensieve: “The Prince’s Tale”

Harry views Snape’s memories and learns:

### Lily Evans and Snape (canon)
- Snape’s childhood friendship with Lily.
- The rupture caused by Snape calling Lily a “Mudblood.”
- Snape’s lifelong regret and enduring love for Lily.

### The prophecy and consequences (canon)
- Snape overhears part of the prophecy and reports it to Voldemort.
- Snape later understands that Lily is in danger because of this.
- Snape pleads with Dumbledore to protect Lily (and later accepts protecting Harry).

### Dumbledore’s trust and the plan (canon)
- Dumbledore relies on Snape for critical tasks.
- The plan includes Snape’s continued cover, the need for Voldemort to trust him, and the prearranged decision around Dumbledore’s death.

### The Patronus (canon)
- Snape’s Patronus is a doe, matching Lily’s.
- The Patronus is shown as evidence of Snape’s enduring attachment to Lily.
- Dumbledore’s line “After all this time?” and Snape’s answer “Always.” appears in this context.

### Harry’s required path (canon)
- The memories clarify what Harry must do and why, including information that reframes the conflict and Harry’s role in it.

**Canon anchors (books):**
- *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows* (The Prince’s Tale)
