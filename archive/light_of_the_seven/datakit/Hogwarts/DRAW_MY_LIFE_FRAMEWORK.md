# Draw My Life: A Visual Biography Framework

> **"The difference between writing a life and drawing a life is the difference between describing a journey and mapping the terrain."**

---

## The Core Question

**How do you "draw" someone's life rather than write it?**

Drawing implies:
- **Visual structure** — shapes, timelines, spatial relationships
- **Compression** — essence over exhaustive detail
- **Emotion made visible** — colors, sizes, connections
- **The whole arc at once** — not sequential reading, but gestalt perception

---

## Learning About a Person: The Five Lenses

To draw someone's life with integrity, you must first understand it. These lenses reveal different facets:

### 1. **Their Work & Creations** 🔨
What they made reveals what they valued.
- Products, art, writings, inventions
- Decisions they made under pressure
- What they chose to spend their time on

*Example (Snape): His potions annotations in the Half-Blood Prince textbook reveal a meticulous, innovative mind hidden behind his cold exterior.*

### 2. **Their Own Words** 🎤
Interviews, autobiographies, letters, journals.
- How they narrate their own story
- What they emphasize vs. omit
- The language they use for key moments

*Example (Snape): "Always." — A single word that reframes an entire life.*

### 3. **Those Who Knew Them** 👥
Perspectives from collaborators, family, rivals.
- Consistent patterns across observers
- Contradictions that reveal complexity
- What others saw that they couldn't see in themselves

*Example (Snape): Dumbledore saw his capacity for love; Harry saw cruelty; Lily saw the boy who could have been different.*

### 4. **The Era & Context** 🌍
No life exists in isolation.
- Historical forces shaping their choices
- Constraints they operated under
- What was possible vs. impossible in their time

*Example (Snape): The First Wizarding War forced a choice between two masters; the Second War demanded he play both sides.*

### 5. **Patterns & Turning Points** 🔄
Recurring themes and pivotal moments.
- What they kept returning to
- Moments where everything changed
- The through-line connecting disparate events

*Example (Snape): The pattern of protective secrecy — hiding his love, his loyalties, his true nature — from Lily's death to his own.*

## The Visual Grammar of a Life

Unlike written biography (linear, sequential), drawn biography uses:

### Temporal Anchors
From the `TemporalPatronus` model:
- **Past Anchor** — formative events that echo forward
- **Present Anchor** — the "now" where consequences manifest
- **Future Anchor** — projected impact, legacy, what outlasts them

### Emotional Topology
From `ancient_magic_lib.py`:
- **Peaks** — moments of triumph, joy, breakthrough
- **Valleys** — loss, failure, despair
- **Plateaus** — periods of stability or stagnation
- **Cliffs** — sudden changes, traumas, revelations

### Relational Geometry
- **Constellations** — clusters of people/events that belong together
- **Orbits** — who stayed close vs. drifted away
- **Collisions** — transformative encounters
- **Parallels** — lives that ran alongside without touching

### Visual Encodings

| Life Element | Visual Representation |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Time passing | Horizontal position (left→right) |
| Emotional intensity | Vertical height or color saturation |
| Importance | Size of element |
| Relationships | Connecting lines (solid=strong, dashed=weak) |
| Turning points | Nodes, stars, or bright markers |
| Themes | Recurring colors or symbols |
| Uncertainty | Blur, transparency, dotted lines |

## The Draw My Life Process

### Phase 1: Gather
- Collect sources across all five lenses
- Note dates, facts, quotes
- Tag emotional valence of each element

### Phase 2: Structure
- Identify 5-7 major life phases
- Mark turning points between phases
- Map relationships active in each phase

### Phase 3: Find the Through-Line
- What is the ONE thing that explains this life?
- What pattern keeps recurring?
- What question were they trying to answer?

### Phase 4: Compose the Visual
- Choose a visual metaphor (journey, tree, constellation, wave)
- Lay out temporal anchors
- Encode emotions, relationships, turning points
- Add the through-line as a visual spine

### Phase 5: Respect & Acknowledge
- Cite sources for claims
- Note uncertainties explicitly
- Acknowledge the limits of external understanding
- Leave room for the mystery of a life

## Example: Severus Snape (Already in Exhibit)

**Through-Line:** Love that could not be expressed, expressed through sacrifice.

**Visual Metaphor:** The Silver Doe — a light that guides without being seen.

**Temporal Anchors:**
- Past: First meeting with Lily
- Present: Casting the Patronus to guide Harry
- Future: The Pensieve revealing all

**Phases:**
1. Childhood (neglect, escape into magic)
2. Hogwarts (friendship, loss, wrong choices)
3. First War (Death Eater, turning point)
4. Interwar (penance, teaching, waiting)
5. Second War (double agent, final sacrifice)

**Visual Encoding:**
- Color: Silver for the doe, green for Slytherin, red for the wound
- Shape: The doe as recurring symbol
- Line: The constant thread of protecting Harry

## Who Is This For?

The "Draw My Life" framework applies to:
- **Fictional characters** — Snape, Slytherin, founders
- **Historical figures** — inventors, artists, scientists
- **Living people** — with their permission and input
- **Yourself** — self-reflection through visual structure

The respect it deserves:
- Every life is irreducibly complex
- No drawing is the full truth
- The goal is insight, not judgment
- Acknowledge what you cannot know
